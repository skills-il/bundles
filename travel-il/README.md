# Flights and Travel from Israel Bundle

Almost every trip out of Israel runs through the same four problems: what the flight really costs once baggage is added, whether your passport needs a visa, what you are owed when the airline cancels, and what customs takes off you on the way home. This bundle puts seven skills across that whole arc, from the fare search to the demand letter.

## Key facts

- **Flight compensation for Israel**: flights departing from or arriving in Israel are governed by the Aviation Services Law (חוק שירותי תעופה, known as the Tibi Law, 2012). The amounts and the qualifying thresholds are **not** the same as the European rules, so the applicable regime has to be identified before any number is quoted.
- **Import taxes**: goods brought into Israel are classified into an **8-digit Israeli HS code**, and the landed cost is customs duty plus **VAT at 18%** plus purchase tax (mas kniya) where it applies. A personal-import exemption threshold applies below a certain value.
- **Exchange rates**: conversions use the Bank of Israel official representative rate (שער יציג), including the historical rate for a specific transaction date, which is what a tax filing needs.
- **Nothing is invented**: the flight skill reads live fares from real search links and never quotes a made-up price; the trip planner checks visa rules and travel warnings against official sources rather than guessing, because both change frequently.
